<section class="fade-in">

  <span class="eyebrow">The Problem</span>

  <h2>
    When policy exists, but the
    <span class="champagne-accent">next step is unclear.</span>
  </h2>

  <p>
    Employees may encounter situations where they need policy guidance quickly
    but are unsure which document applies, whether the information they found
    is current, or who owns the policy.
  </p>

  <p>
    That uncertainty becomes especially important when an employee reports
    something that could represent a security incident, such as clicking a
    suspicious or phishing-related link. In those situations, the experience
    should move beyond ordinary policy lookup and guide the employee toward
    approved incident-reporting and escalation steps.
  </p>

</section>


<section class="fade-in">

  <span class="eyebrow">Planned MVP Workflow</span>

  <h2>
    From employee question to
    <span class="champagne-accent">appropriate action.</span>
  </h2>

  <p>
    The planned MVP allows an employee to submit a question and receive an
    answer based on approved company policy information. The experience is
    designed to show the relevant policy source and provide access to the
    appropriate policy owner or department contact.
  </p>

  <p>
    When a question cannot be answered confidently, the workflow is designed
    to support clarification instead of presenting uncertain information as
    fact. Employees can be guided toward the correct contact, and separate
    email workflows can support clarification requests and policy-owner
    notifications when necessary.
  </p>

</section>

<section class="fade-in">

  <span class="eyebrow">Inside the MVP</span>

  <h2>
    A closer look at the
    <span class="champagne-accent">core experience.</span>
  </h2>

  <div style="margin-top: 3rem;">

    <div style="margin-bottom: 3rem;">
      <h3 class="champagne-accent">01</h3>
      <h3>Policy Management</h3>

      <p>
        DeRexi is designed to organize approved company policies and maintain
        trusted policy information so employees are guided by the correct
        organizational sources. This creates the foundation for reliable
        retrieval, ownership tracking, and future version management.
      </p>
    </div>


    <div style="margin-bottom: 3rem;">
      <h3 class="champagne-accent">02</h3>
      <h3>Policy Search & Retrieval</h3>

      <p>
        Employees can ask questions in everyday language while DeRexi works to
        identify the most relevant approved policy guidance and supporting
        source, reducing the need to search through multiple documents manually.
      </p>
    </div>


    <div style="margin-bottom: 3rem;">
      <h3 class="champagne-accent">03</h3>
      <h3>Clarification & Routing</h3>

      <p>
        When DeRexi cannot confidently resolve a question, the workflow is
        designed to avoid guessing. Instead, employees can see the appropriate
        policy owner or department and use dedicated clarification workflows to
        reach the right person.
      </p>
    </div>


    <div style="margin-bottom: 3rem;">
      <h3 class="champagne-accent">04</h3>
      <h3>Incident Assistance</h3>

        escalation guidance.
      </p>
    </div>


    <div style="margin-bottom: 3rem;">
      <h3 class="champagne-accent">05</h3>

<section class="fade-in">

  <span class="eyebrow">What I'm Learning</span>

  <h2>
    Designing security around
    <span class="champagne-accent">people, process & technology.</span>
  </h2>

  <p>
    Developing DeRexi is helping me think beyond a single technical feature and
    consider how multiple parts of a security solution work together. The
    project includes requirements gathering, workflow design, policy
    governance, data organization, access considerations, user experience,
    incident-response thinking, and future testing.
  </p>

  <p>
    It is also reinforcing an important part of my approach to cybersecurity:
    effective security guidance should help people understand what to do next,
    not simply give them more information to interpret on their own.
  </p>
