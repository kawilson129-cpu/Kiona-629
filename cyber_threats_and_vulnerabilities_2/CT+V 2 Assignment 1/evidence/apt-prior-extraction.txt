   SCENARIO STATUS FICTIONAL / EDUCATIONAL. DeRexi and the compromise described in this report are fictional. APT38's
   capabilities and ATT&CK; mappings are grounded in public CISA and MITRE reporting; the scenario applies those documented
   behaviors to a hypothetical banking-policy platform.
Organization: DeRexi: Policy Pilot - a policy-governance platform used by banks and financial institutions to store, retrieve and
operationalize internal policies and procedures.
Threat actor: APT38 / BeagleBoyz, a North Korean state-sponsored cluster associated with financially motivated operations
against banks, SWIFT endpoints and ATMs.
Incident premise: APT38 targets DeRexi as a strategic third-party access point. Rather than attacking one bank at a time, the
actor attempts to compromise DeRexi's internal environment to learn how client institutions operate, obtain privileged access,
and create opportunities for downstream financial fraud.

01 // EXECUTIVE INCIDENT SUMMARY

What happened to DeRexi?

In this fictional scenario, a DeRexi employee receives a convincing spearphishing email disguised as a banking-client policy
review request. The attached document is malicious. When opened, it initiates attacker-controlled execution on the employee
workstation. APT38 then uses built-in Windows tooling to execute commands, establishes persistence, attempts to obtain
additional account access, and begins discovering DeRexi's files and internal network resources.
The attackers' objective is not simply to steal DeRexi documents. Because DeRexi services financial institutions, its
environment contains valuable operational context: policy-owner directories, escalation procedures, authentication workflows,
incident-response guidance, and potentially client-specific policy repositories. That knowledge could help an adversary
understand where financial controls exist and how employees are instructed to respond to suspicious activity.
As the intrusion progresses, the actor collects useful local data and attempts to reduce forensic evidence by deleting artifacts
and modifying timestamps. The security team detects unusual PowerShell activity, abnormal file/share enumeration and
suspicious service creation. DeRexi isolates the affected systems before evidence confirms any successful compromise of a
client bank.

   INCIDENT SEVERITY HIGH. The hypothetical incident creates both direct risk to DeRexi and supply-chain risk to banking
   clients. No client compromise is assumed in this report; the scenario stops at a confirmed DeRexi intrusion with potential
   downstream exposure.

02 // WHY APT38 WOULD TARGET DEREXI

The banking connection

MITRE describes APT38 as a North Korean state-sponsored group specializing in financial cyber operations and documents
targeting of banks, financial institutions, SWIFT endpoints and ATMs. CISA's FASTCash 2.0 advisory describes BeagleBoyz
activity against financial institutions and warns of operational risk beyond direct theft.

A policy-governance vendor serving banks could be attractive because policy information reveals how organizations are
expected to operate. Even when policy documents do not contain passwords, they can expose security processes, approval
chains, privileged roles, incident escalation paths, payment controls and terminology used inside client organizations.

   THREAT MODEL The fictional motive is strategic access and intelligence: compromise the platform provider, understand client
   procedures, seek credentials or privileged pathways, and use that knowledge to support later financial operations. This is an
   analytical scenario - not a claim that APT38 has targeted DeRexi or an equivalent real product.

Potentially exposed DeRexi assets

Asset                              Why an attacker may value it

Policy repository                  Reveals security, payment, access-control and incident procedures.

Employee / policy-owner directory  Identifies roles, approvers, administrators and escalation contacts.

Authentication environment         May provide a path toward privileged DeRexi accounts if compromised.

Audit / usage data                 Shows which policies, systems or procedures are important to client operations.

Client configuration metadata      Could help an attacker distinguish customers and understand platform relationships.

What the compromise could mean

Risk area                       Potential consequence

Confidentiality                 Unauthorized access to internal or client policy information, directories and operational metadata.

Integrity                       Attackers could attempt to alter policy content, guidance or configuration, creating dangerous misinformation for
                                users.

Availability                    Containment may require taking portions of DeRexi offline, interrupting client access to policy guidance.

Credential / identity risk      Compromised accounts could allow the actor to impersonate employees or administrators.

Banking client risk             Knowledge of client controls and procedures could support downstream social engineering or intrusion attempts.

Regulatory / reputational risk  A compromise of a policy platform used by regulated financial institutions could trigger notification, audit and trust
                                concerns.

Why integrity is especially important for DeRexi

DeRexi exists to help employees obtain trustworthy policy guidance. That makes content integrity a security requirement, not
merely a documentation concern. If an attacker altered an incident-response instruction, approval threshold, payment
procedure or escalation contact, a user could confidently follow information that is technically available but no longer
trustworthy.

06 // RESPONSE & HARDENING

How DeRexi responds

Contain. Isolate affected endpoints, revoke sessions, disable suspected accounts and restrict administrative access.
Preserve evidence. Centralize endpoint, identity, application and cloud logs so attacker deletion on one host does not erase
the investigation trail.
Validate policy integrity. Compare policy documents and versions against trusted hashes/version history and identify
unauthorized changes.
Protect privileged access. Require phishing-resistant MFA and separate administrative identities from normal user accounts.
Detect APT38-style behaviors. Alert on suspicious PowerShell, new services, repeated authentication attempts, unusual
share enumeration, file deletion and timestamp anomalies.
Segment clients. Architect tenant and administrative boundaries so compromise of one employee or component cannot
become unrestricted access to every banking customer.
Coordinate banking notifications. Use predefined incident escalation and client communication procedures when a
compromise could affect regulated financial institutions.

   POLICY PILOT CONNECTION This fictional incident reinforces DeRexi's own product purpose: during a high-pressure security
   event, employees need fast, current and trustworthy procedures. The platform must therefore protect not only access to
   policies, but the integrity, version history and ownership of the guidance itself.

07 // CONCLUSION & EVIDENCE BASIS

Incident conclusion

This fictional case study places a real financial-sector threat actor into the environment DeRexi is designed to serve. APT38 /
BeagleBoyz is a credible adversary model because public reporting documents its focus on financial institutions and its use of
techniques spanning phishing, command execution, persistence, credential attacks, discovery, collection and defense evasion.
The scenario demonstrates how MITRE ATT&CK; can turn a fictional incident into a structured, defensible analysis. Instead of
saying only that 'DeRexi was hacked,' the report identifies the attacker's objective at each stage, the technique used to achieve
it, the evidence defenders would expect to observe and the controls that could interrupt the attack.
Most importantly, the scenario shows why a policy-governance provider serving banks must be treated as part of the
financial-sector security ecosystem. Protecting policy data, privileged access, audit trails and client separation is essential
because compromise of a trusted platform can create risk beyond the vendor itself.

EVIDENCE BASIS

CISA AA20-239A - FASTCash 2.0: North Korea's BeagleBoyz Robbing Banks. CISA, Treasury, FBI and USCYBERCOM describe North Korean
bank-targeting activity, FASTCash ATM cash-outs, SWIFT abuse, malware/IOCs and risks to the Financial Services sector.
MITRE ATT&CK; - APT38 (G0082). MITRE identifies APT38 as a North Korean state-sponsored group specializing in financial cyber operations, lists
BeagleBoyz as an associated group, and documents the techniques used as the evidence basis for this fictional mapping.
