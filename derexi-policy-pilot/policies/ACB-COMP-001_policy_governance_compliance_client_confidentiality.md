## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how Aurum Capital Bank creates, reviews, approves, publishes, and maintains its internal policies, and how employees request clarification when policy language is unclear. It also sets out the bank's rules for protecting client account confidentiality and for preparing records and evidence for internal compliance reviews and audits.

This is an internal Aurum Capital Bank policy describing bank rules and internal procedures. It supports the bank's obligations under the GLBA / Regulation P privacy requirements and the GLBA Safeguards Rule; where a legal or regulatory requirement is stricter, the stricter requirement applies.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all Aurum Capital Bank employees, contractors, and policy owners. It covers policy creation and review, approval and publication, document version control, policy owner responsibilities, employee clarification requests, ambiguous policy language, internal compliance review preparation, audit evidence and documentation, client account confidentiality, authorized information sharing, and confidentiality incident escalation.

## 3. Policy Requirements

### 3.1 Policy creation and review
- Every policy must name a policy owner, a department, a version number, an effective date, and a review date.
- Policies are reviewed at least annually and whenever a related law, regulation, or business process changes.
- New or substantially revised policies must be reviewed by Compliance before approval.
- Policies must be written in clear, employee-facing language so that employees can follow them without special training.

### 3.2 Policy approval and publication
- Policies must be approved before they are used. The approval authority depends on the policy's subject matter; Compliance approves policies that carry regulatory or client-confidentiality obligations.
- Approved policies are published in the central policy library, where employees can search and retrieve the current version.
- Only the approved version may be relied upon for bank work. Draft and retired versions are not authoritative.
- Superseded versions are retained for audit history but must be clearly marked as superseded.

### 3.3 Document version control
- Policy text is versioned, not overwritten. Each change creates a new version with a new version number and effective date.
- The current version is the most recent approved version that has taken effect.
- Version history and effective dates must be retained so the bank can show what the policy said at any point in time.
- Employees must always follow the current approved version.

### 3.4 Policy owner responsibilities
- The policy owner maintains the policy, keeps it accurate and current, and answers employee questions about it.
- Owners must review their policies on schedule (at least annually) and update them when the law, regulation, or process changes.
- Owners must respond to clarification requests routed to them and document the outcome.
- When employees repeatedly misunderstand a policy, the owner must determine whether the policy text itself is unclear and revise it if so.

### 3.5 Employee clarification requests
- Employees who do not understand a policy, or who face a situation the policy does not clearly address, must request clarification rather than guess.
- Clarification requests are routed to the responsible policy owner, who responds and, if needed, updates the policy.
- The DeRexi assistant routes questions it cannot answer confidently to the appropriate policy owner.
- Employees must not rely on informal explanations or outdated documents for policy decisions.

### 3.6 Handling ambiguous policy language
- When a policy is ambiguous, the policy owner must interpret it consistently and document the interpretation.
- If employees repeatedly make the same mistake or ask the same question, the owner must treat that as a signal that the policy language is unclear and revise it.
- Repeated misunderstanding of a policy is a policy-quality issue, not only an employee issue. Owners must correct the language, add examples, and communicate the clarification to affected teams.
- If an ambiguity could affect client confidentiality, fair lending, or a regulatory obligation, the owner must involve Compliance.

### 3.7 Internal compliance review preparation
- When an internal compliance review or audit is announced, policy owners must gather the current approved policy, its version history, effective dates, and evidence that employees have been informed.
- Records must be complete, accurate, and organized by policy so a reviewer can trace each requirement to its evidence.
- Do not alter, backdate, or delete records in preparation for a review. Provide records as they exist.
- If a record is missing, document the gap and notify Compliance rather than recreating the record.

### 3.8 Audit evidence and documentation
- Evidence includes policy versions, approval records, effective dates, training or acknowledgment records, and clarification requests and responses.
- Evidence must be retained for the period required by the bank's retention schedule and applicable law.
- Evidence must be stored in approved systems and be retrievable by policy and date.
- Employees must cooperate with Compliance and internal audit requests.

### 3.9 Client account confidentiality
- Client account information is confidential and must be protected under GLBA / Regulation P and the GLBA Safeguards Rule.
- **Employees must not disclose client account details—names, account numbers, balances, holdings, or transactions—to anyone who does not have a legitimate business need to know.**
- Client account information must be shared only through approved systems and channels, and only for a permitted business purpose.
- Client account information must not be discussed in public places, in open offices where others can overhear, or with family or friends.

### 3.10 Authorized information sharing
- Sharing client information internally is permitted only with employees who have a need to know for a legitimate business purpose.
- Sharing with anyone outside the bank is permitted only through an approved process (for example, a regulatory request, a client-authorized disclosure, or a vendor under contract).
- When sharing in a meeting, confirm that every attendee has a need to know before account-specific details are discussed.
- If non-essential attendees are present, remove or redact client account details.

### 3.11 Confidentiality incident escalation
- Any suspected improper disclosure of client or bank information must be reported immediately to Compliance and Information Security.
- Do not attempt to retrieve or "undo" a disclosure on your own; report it and follow instructions.
- Compliance will determine whether notification obligations apply.

## 4. Employee Procedures

### 4.1 To prepare policy records for an internal compliance review
1. Identify each policy in scope and open its current approved version.
2. Gather the version history, effective dates, and approval records.
3. Collect evidence that employees were informed (acknowledgments, training records).
4. Organize the records by policy so each requirement maps to evidence.
5. Provide the records as they exist; do not alter or recreate them.
6. Document and report any gaps to Compliance.

### 4.2 When employees repeatedly misunderstand a policy
1. Confirm what employees are getting wrong and why.
2. Review the policy language for ambiguity or missing examples.
3. Interpret the policy consistently and document the interpretation.
4. Revise the policy to remove the ambiguity and add examples where helpful.
5. Communicate the clarification to affected teams, with Compliance involvement if confidentiality, fair lending, or a regulatory obligation is involved.

### 4.3 To request clarification of a policy
1. Identify the policy and the specific language that is unclear.
2. Submit a clarification request to the policy owner (or through DeRexi).
3. Do not guess in the meantime; follow the clearest reasonable reading and note the open question.
4. Keep the owner's answer with your records.

### 4.4 To share client account information
1. Confirm the business purpose for sharing.
2. Confirm that everyone who will receive the information has a need to know.
3. Use an approved system or channel.
4. If others are present who do not need to know, remove or redact account details, or defer the discussion.
5. If information is disclosed improperly, report it to Compliance and Information Security immediately.

## 6. Exceptions & Escalation

Exceptions to policy governance requirements must be approved by Compliance. Ambiguities that affect client confidentiality, fair lending, or a regulatory obligation must be escalated to Compliance. Any suspected improper disclosure of client or bank information must be reported immediately to Compliance and Information Security; do not attempt to resolve it yourself.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I prepare policy records for an internal compliance review?**
Open each policy in scope, gather its current approved version, version history, effective dates, and approval records, then collect evidence that employees were informed (acknowledgments and training records). Organize everything by policy so each requirement maps to its evidence. Provide records exactly as they exist—do not alter, backdate, or recreate them—and report any gaps to Compliance.

**What should I do when employees repeatedly misunderstand a policy?**
Treat it as a policy-quality signal, not just an employee problem. The policy owner should review the language, interpret the policy consistently, revise the text to remove the ambiguity, add examples, and communicate the clarification to affected teams. Involve Compliance if confidentiality, fair lending, or a regulatory obligation is affected.

**Can I share client account details during an internal meeting?**
Only if every attendee has a legitimate need to know and the information is shared through an approved channel for a permitted business purpose. If anyone present does not need to know, remove or redact the account details or defer the discussion. Discussing client accounts in public places or with people who do not need to know is prohibited.

**How do I request clarification of unclear policy language?**
Submit a clarification request to the responsible policy owner or through the DeRexi assistant. Do not guess; follow the clearest reasonable reading and record the open question and the owner's answer.

**I may have shared client information by mistake. What do I do?**
Report it immediately to Compliance and Information Security. Do not try to retrieve or undo the disclosure yourself; follow their instructions and document what happened.
